# High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

## Overview

High blood pressure, also called hypertension, occurs when the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries is too high.

A blood pressure reading has two numbers:

- Systolic pressure: the pressure when the heart beats.
- Diastolic pressure: the pressure when the heart is resting between beats.

For example, a blood pressure reading of 120/80 means a systolic pressure of 120 and a diastolic pressure of 80.

## Symptoms

High blood pressure usually has no symptoms. Regular blood pressure measurements are therefore important for detecting it.

Very high blood pressure can be dangerous and may require immediate medical attention.

## Blood Pressure Categories

For adults, MedlinePlus lists the following categories:

- Normal: systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80.
- Elevated: systolic 120–129 and diastolic below 80.
- Stage 1 high blood pressure: systolic 130–139 or diastolic 80–89.
- Stage 2 high blood pressure: systolic 140 or higher or diastolic 90 or higher.
- Hypertensive crisis: systolic higher than 180 and diastolic higher than 120. Seek medical care right away.

A healthcare provider considers multiple blood pressure measurements when diagnosing hypertension.

## Types

There are two main types of high blood pressure:

### Primary hypertension

Primary, or essential, hypertension is the most common type. It generally develops over time, often as people get older.

### Secondary hypertension

Secondary hypertension is caused by another medical condition or by certain medicines. Treating the underlying condition or stopping the causative medicine may improve the blood pressure.

## Risk Factors and Causes

Factors associated with high blood pressure can include:

- Older age
- Family history
- Obesity
- High sodium intake
- Physical inactivity
- Diabetes
- Tobacco use
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Certain medical conditions
- Certain medicines

In many people with primary hypertension, no single specific cause can be identified.

## Health Risks

Persistently high blood pressure can increase the risk of serious health problems, including:

- Heart attack
- Stroke
- Heart failure
- Kidney disease
- Eye problems

## Diagnosis

High blood pressure is diagnosed using blood pressure measurements.

A healthcare provider may take multiple readings at different appointments before making a diagnosis.

Blood pressure can vary depending on factors such as the time of day and other circumstances, so a single measurement does not necessarily establish a diagnosis.

## Treatment

Treatment can include:

- Heart-healthy lifestyle changes
- Physical activity
- Healthy eating
- Reducing sodium intake
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Managing stress
- Medicines when appropriate

The appropriate treatment plan depends on the person's blood pressure and other health factors. Treatment decisions should be made with a healthcare provider.

## Important Safety Note

This document provides general educational information. It is not a diagnosis or individualized medical advice. A healthcare professional should be consulted for diagnosis and treatment decisions.
