# Overview

Pneumonia is an infection in one or both lungs. It causes the air sacs in the lungs to fill with fluid or pus.

Pneumonia can range from mild to severe. The severity depends on factors such as the organism causing the infection, the person's age, and overall health.

Pneumonia can affect people of any age.

# Causes

Pneumonia can be caused by different types of germs, including:

- Bacteria
- Viruses
- Fungi

Bacterial pneumonia is a common cause of pneumonia in adults.

Viral infections of the respiratory tract can also cause pneumonia. Viruses associated with pneumonia include influenza viruses, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), and SARS-CoV-2.

Fungal pneumonia is more common in people with weakened immune systems or certain chronic health conditions.

Pneumonia can also occur after accidentally breathing food, liquid, vomit, or other material into the lungs. This is called aspiration pneumonia.

# Types of Pneumonia

Pneumonia can be classified in several ways depending on its cause and where it was acquired.

Examples include:

- Bacterial pneumonia
- Viral pneumonia
- Fungal pneumonia
- Aspiration pneumonia
- Community-acquired pneumonia
- Hospital-acquired pneumonia

Different types may require different approaches to diagnosis and treatment.

# How Pneumonia Can Develop

Pneumonia can develop when disease-causing organisms reach the lungs.

Bacteria or viruses that are present in the nose, mouth, or upper respiratory tract can sometimes spread into the lungs.

A person can also inhale infectious particles directly.

Aspiration can occur when food, liquids, vomit, or secretions enter the lungs instead of passing normally through the digestive tract.

# Risk Factors

Anyone can develop pneumonia, but certain factors increase the risk.

Risk factors include:

- Being age 65 or older
- Being age 2 or younger
- Smoking
- Heavy alcohol use
- Malnutrition
- Chronic lung disease
- Heart disease
- Diabetes
- Liver disease
- Kidney disease
- A weakened immune system
- Difficulty coughing or swallowing
- Recent respiratory infection
- Recent surgery or trauma
- Exposure to certain chemicals, pollutants, or toxic fumes
- Recent hospitalization

People who are hospitalized, particularly those in intensive care, may have an increased risk of pneumonia.

# Symptoms

Pneumonia symptoms can range from mild to severe.

Common symptoms include:

- Cough
- Fever
- Chills
- Shortness of breath
- Chest pain when breathing or coughing
- Fatigue

Other symptoms can include:

- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Headache
- Loss of appetite
- Weakness
- Sweating

A cough may produce mucus or phlegm.

# Symptoms in Older Adults

Older adults may have different or less obvious symptoms.

They may have:

- Weakness
- Confusion
- Reduced alertness
- Lower-than-normal body temperature

Older adults and people with serious illnesses or weakened immune systems may have fewer typical symptoms even when pneumonia is serious.

# Symptoms in Infants and Children

Newborns and infants may not show typical symptoms.

Children may have:

- Fever
- Cough
- Vomiting
- Difficulty breathing
- Reduced energy
- Restlessness

Symptoms vary depending on the child's age and the cause of the infection.

# Diagnosis

Pneumonia can sometimes be difficult to distinguish from other respiratory infections because symptoms can overlap with conditions such as a cold or influenza.

A health care provider may:

- Ask about symptoms and medical history
- Perform a physical examination
- Listen to the lungs with a stethoscope
- Order imaging or laboratory tests

# Chest X-Ray

A chest X-ray is commonly used when pneumonia is suspected.

It can help show areas of infection or inflammation in the lungs.

# Blood Tests

Blood tests may be used to evaluate infection and overall health.

A complete blood count (CBC) may be performed.

Blood cultures may sometimes be used to determine whether bacteria have entered the bloodstream.

# Sputum Testing

A sputum test examines mucus produced by coughing.

It may help identify bacteria or other organisms causing pneumonia.

Sputum testing is more likely to be used when pneumonia is severe or when identifying the specific organism is important for treatment.

# Additional Tests

People who are hospitalized, have severe symptoms, are older, or have other serious health problems may need additional tests.

These may include:

- Chest CT scan
- Pulse oximetry
- Pleural fluid testing
- Bronchoscopy

Pulse oximetry measures the amount of oxygen in the blood.

# Treatment

Treatment depends on:

- The cause of the pneumonia
- The type of pneumonia
- The severity of illness
- The person's age
- Other health conditions

## Bacterial Pneumonia

Antibiotics are used to treat bacterial pneumonia.

The choice of antibiotic depends on the suspected or identified bacteria and the person's medical situation.

## Viral Pneumonia

Antibiotics do not treat viral pneumonia.

Some viral pneumonias may be treated with antiviral medicines depending on the virus and the person's circumstances.

## Fungal Pneumonia

Fungal pneumonia can require antifungal medicines.

The appropriate treatment depends on the specific fungus and the person's health condition.

# Hospital Treatment

Some people with pneumonia need treatment in a hospital.

Hospital care may be necessary when symptoms are severe or when a person has a high risk of complications.

Hospital treatment may include:

- Oxygen therapy
- Intravenous medicines
- Fluids
- Monitoring of oxygen levels
- Respiratory support

Severe cases may require mechanical ventilation.

# Recovery

Recovery time varies.

Some people begin to feel better within about a week.

For others, recovery can take a month or longer.

Older adults and people with chronic diseases or weakened immune systems may take longer to recover.

Fatigue or cough may continue for some time after the infection improves.

# Complications

Pneumonia can sometimes cause serious complications.

Possible complications include:

- Bacteremia
- Sepsis or septic shock
- Lung abscess
- Pleural disorders
- Pleural effusion
- Kidney failure
- Respiratory failure

The risk of complications is higher in certain people, including older adults, very young children, people with weakened immune systems, and people with serious chronic diseases.

# Pneumonia and Respiratory Failure

Severe pneumonia can interfere with the lungs' ability to provide enough oxygen to the body.

Respiratory failure can occur when the lungs cannot adequately exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.

People with respiratory failure may require oxygen therapy or mechanical ventilation.

# Prevention

Some types of pneumonia can be prevented or their risk reduced.

Preventive measures include:

- Staying up to date with recommended vaccines
- Getting recommended influenza vaccination
- Asking a health care provider about pneumococcal vaccination when appropriate
- Following recommended COVID-19 vaccination guidance
- Washing hands frequently
- Avoiding tobacco smoke
- Maintaining a healthy lifestyle
- Avoiding close contact with people who are sick when possible

Vaccination recommendations depend on factors such as age and health conditions.

# Pneumonia and Smoking

Smoking damages the lungs and can make it more difficult for the respiratory system to fight infection.

Not smoking can reduce the risk of respiratory infections and other lung problems.

People who smoke can discuss smoking cessation support with a health care professional.

# Pneumonia and Chronic Conditions

Certain chronic conditions can increase the risk of severe pneumonia.

Examples include:

- Diabetes
- Heart disease
- Chronic lung disease
- Liver disease
- Kidney disease
- Conditions that weaken the immune system

People with these conditions may need additional preventive measures and medical monitoring.

# When to Seek Medical Care

Medical evaluation may be needed when a person has:

- Persistent or worsening cough
- Difficulty breathing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest pain that occurs with breathing or coughing
- Persistent fever
- Shaking chills
- Coughing up blood
- Symptoms that become worse after initially improving

People with weakened immune systems should seek medical advice promptly when symptoms of pneumonia occur.

# Emergency Warning Signs

Severe difficulty breathing can be an emergency.

Urgent medical care is needed when breathing becomes increasingly difficult, when a person becomes confused or unusually sleepy, or when severe symptoms occur.

Blue or gray discoloration of the lips or fingertips can indicate inadequate oxygen and requires urgent medical attention.

# Important Safety Note

This document provides general medical information for educational purposes.

It does not diagnose pneumonia or determine which organism is causing an infection.

Antibiotics, antivirals, antifungals, oxygen therapy, and other treatments should only be selected with appropriate medical guidance.

Difficulty breathing, severe chest symptoms, confusion, or other severe symptoms require prompt medical evaluation.
